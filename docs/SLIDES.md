## 2 — The shift

# The next company runs on AI workers.

Not just humans and contractors — a **workforce of autonomous agents**:
research, growth, procurement, engineering.

They already spend, hire, and coordinate. On their own.

---

## 3 — The problem

# How do you manage a workforce you can't see?

- Which workers are reliable?
- Which waste money?
- Which deserve bigger budgets?
- Which can be trusted to hire others?

Today: you hand an agent a key and hope.

---

## 4 — Sentinel

# The operating system for that workforce.

- **Hire** — scoped budget, per-tx limit, expiry, categories
- **Govern** — every payment checked before value moves
- **Score** — explainable, deterministic trust
- **Allocate** — trust decides autonomy *and* capital

---

## 5 — Govern + settle *(live)*

# Guardrails are real. Trust is earned.

1. Pause a worker → it tries to pay → **blocked.** Reliability dips.
2. Resume → **real x402 USDC settlement on Solana.** Trust rises — live.

*Every change explainable: `Trust +3 · Successful x402 settlement`.*

---

## 6 — Workers hiring workers *(live)*

# A company, not a tool.

A trusted worker delegates to another — trust-aware, within its authority.
*(Supervised workers can't delegate. Trust gates autonomy.)*

A payment edge animates in. Both reputations evolve.

---

## 7 — Trust → capital

# Reliability allocates the budget.

| Factor | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Payment reliability | 40% |
| Spending discipline | 25% |
| Task completion | 20% |
| Consistency | 15% |

Autonomy tiers + budget recommendations. The reliable worker earns more.

---

## 8 — Vision

# Trust becomes the operating system.

As companies are composed of AI workers, budgets are allocated algorithmically,
and agents collaborate economically — someone has to govern them.

**This is what managing an AI-native company looks like.**
